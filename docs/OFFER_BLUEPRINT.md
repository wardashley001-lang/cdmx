# La Güera's Guide: Offer Blueprint

Output of a six-stage offer interview (story, skills, audience, offer design, pricing, positioning), plus market research. Written 2026-10-29. Personal financial, legal, and third-party details from the interview are deliberately left out of this file.

## 1. Story (Stage 1)

- Ashley moved from Scottsdale, AZ to Mexico City at 32. She had a good career, an apartment, and a new car, but her life felt over-structured and joyless.
- She moved alone and kept her remote job.
- Early challenges: first long solo stint abroad, family skepticism, loneliness, the language, and safety fears. The safety fear reversed once she arrived.
- Now: the biggest community of her adult life, a full social calendar, and a Condesa apartment.
- Known for: pink, fashion, fun, "high maintenance," a friend who always knows where to go, and work in private equity that people don't expect from a blonde.
- What people DM about: shock at how cosmopolitan Mexico City is, restaurant and event recommendations, and jokes about safety and cartels.

## 2. Skills (Stage 2)

- Strong lived experience of relocating and building community abroad.
- Insider access to Mexico City nightlife and restaurants through a close Mexican friend group that includes club owners.
- Professional skills: business development, lead sourcing and outreach, sales, project management, AI and tech.
- Not yet proven: no paid clients or coaching results. The food blog hasn't launched. Proof so far is the lived story, friends who used her recommendations, and Instagram engagement.
- Most enjoyed teaching: where to eat, drink, and go.

## 3. Audience (Stage 3)

- About 4,200 Instagram followers, ages 20–45, in the US and Mexico, mostly women. Higher income than average. Fun and outgoing.
- Many feel the way she did before the move: successful but stuck. The blockers are fear and the practical logistics of remote work.
- Friends have visited and one wants to move.
- The thing they would most want from her is a Mexico City guide.
- She is not active on Facebook. The audience is on Instagram, with TikTok and Pinterest as growth channels.

## 4. Offer design (Stage 4)

- Format: digital, instant delivery, fully hands-off.
- Core promise: "I help social, stylish people skip the research and the tourist traps and go where locals actually go in Mexico City."
- Buyer: visitors, digital nomads, and short-term expats.
- Edge: a live map of 276 spots and counting, and knowledge of new places that exist only on Instagram and aren't on Google Maps yet.
- Capacity: a few hours a week, with as much automation as possible.
- A $3,000+ price point doesn't fit this offer. A volume product fits better.

## 5. Pricing (Stage 5)

Comparable listings found (listing prices, not sales data):

- Mexico City PDF guides list at roughly [$6–$20](https://payhip.com/b/hdJeu).
- Etsy travel guides have a median near [$9](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/travel-guide).
- A free CDMX guide exists from [Mexico Travel & Leisure](https://www.mexicotravelandleisure.com/guides/mexico-city-guide/).
- Closest competitors are map-style food guides on [Gumroad](https://foodhuntermaps.gumroad.com/l/cdmx), [Thatch](https://thatch.co/guide/1gyjuspjv0fbz/view), and [Salt & Wind](https://travel.saltandwind.com/travel-shop/mexico-city-travel-guide). Their prices could not be confirmed.

Working price range: **$15–$25**. Test $15 against $25.

## 6. Positioning (Stage 6)

- Character: the blonde private-equity girl who moved to CDMX and always has the reservation. Voice rules live in `VOICE.md`.
- Hook ideas:
  - "Everyone's going to Pujol. Here's where I send my friends instead."
  - "Mexico City has a new restaurant every week. Half of them aren't on Google."
  - "I moved here alone at 32. Here's the part nobody tells you."
- Answer to "I can just Google it": you can, and you'll spend hours on it and still end up in the wrong places.
- Platforms: Instagram and TikTok first, Pinterest for evergreen search traffic, Facebook later if at all.
- Call to action (vendor-sourced data, so test it yourself):
  - Instagram: "Comment MAP and I'll send it," with a bio link as backup.
  - TikTok: bio link plus a pinned comment.
  - Pinterest: pins that link straight to the sales page.
  - Use a separate link per platform to track what sells.
  - Research: [bio link vs. DM automation](https://creatorflow.so/blog/bio-link-vs-dm-automation-affiliate-clicks/), [comment-to-DM](https://zorcha.com/blogs/why-comment-to-dm-automation-is-replacing-traditional-link-in-bio-strategies/).

## 7. Free vs. paid

The current site is fully public, and `places.json` is downloadable from the page, so there is no reason to pay yet.

**Free (marketing):** Instagram content and the Highlight, a public map of about 20–25 top spots, and the "Hot ahorita" section.

**Paid (about $15–$25, sold as a separate download rather than by locking the site):**

| Piece | Why people pay | What's needed |
|---|---|---|
| Itineraries (3–4: first night out, girls' weekend, Roma/Condesa day, foodie 48 hours) | They skip the decisions | Ashley's real routes |
| Full list saved to their own phone (Google/Apple Maps list) | Convenience | Built from the CSVs |
| Insider notes (how to get in, what to order, when to go) | Access | Real, verified details only |
| Monthly "new this month" drop | Instagram-only openings not on Google yet | About 1–2 hours a month |
| First-timer kit (safety, Ubers, tipping, what to wear) | People already ask this | Ashley's knowledge |

Recommended launch: itineraries + saveable full list + insider notes at about $19, with the monthly drop as a $25 tier.
